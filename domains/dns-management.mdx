Once you have [created a DNS user and set a password](/domains/anycast-dns-user), you can manage DNS records for all domains in your EDIS account through the PowerDNS-Admin portal. This portal lets you add, edit, and delete individual DNS records, control TTLs, toggle record status, and publish your changes to the live DNS.

## Log In and Navigate to Your Zone

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the DNS portal">
    Go to [pdns.edis.global/login](https://pdns.edis.global/login) and sign in with your 5-digit customer number and your DNS portal password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select your domain">
    The portal lists all domains associated with your EDIS account that have DNS zones managed here. Click the domain you want to manage to open its zone editor.

    <Note>
      If a domain you expect to see is missing from the list, its DNS may not be configured to use EDIS nameservers, or the domain may not be assigned to your account. Contact [hosting@edis.at](mailto:hosting@edis.at) with your domain name and customer number.
    </Note>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Understanding the Record Fields

Each row in the zone editor represents one DNS record. The columns work as follows:

### Name (Subdomain)

The **Name** field contains the subdomain part of the hostname — **not** the full domain name. Because you are editing a zone that already belongs to your domain, the system appends the domain automatically.

Enter only the subdomain label. For example:

| You want to create | Enter in Name |
|---|---|
| `www.example.at` | `www` |
| `mail.example.at` | `mail` |
| `s1._domainkey.example.at` | `s1._domainkey` |
| The root domain `example.at` itself | `@` |
| A wildcard for all subdomains | `*` |

<Warning>
  Do **not** include the domain name itself in the Name field. If you enter `s1._domainkey.example.at` instead of `s1._domainkey`, the resulting record will resolve to `s1._domainkey.example.at.example.at` — which is incorrect and will break the entry.
</Warning>

**The `@` symbol** represents the root domain (e.g., `example.at` itself) without needing to type out the full domain name each time.

**The `*` wildcard** entry matches subdomains that do not have their own explicit record. A wildcard does not override existing records and does not automatically extend to website certificates — you need to verify that your hosting and SSL configuration actually supports the hostnames the wildcard covers.

### Hostnames

Labels like `mail` or `www` in the Name column are shorthand for subdomains within the zone. The entry `mail` in the zone `example.at` resolves as `mail.example.at`.

### Type

The record type defines what kind of DNS information the record holds. See the [DNS Record Types reference](/domains/dns-records-detail) for a full explanation of A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, and TXT records.

<Note>
  Do not modify or delete the `@ IN NS ns1.edis.global` and `@ IN NS ns2.edis.global` entries. These nameserver (NS) records are required for your domain to resolve correctly through EDIS DNS.
</Note>

### Status

The **Status** column controls whether a record is active:

- **Active** — the record is included in DNS responses
- **Disabled** — the record is ignored in DNS responses but remains saved in the portal

Use Disabled to temporarily remove a record without deleting it.

### TTL (Time to Live)

**TTL** is measured in seconds and tells resolvers how long they may cache the record. A TTL of `3600` means resolvers can keep the cached answer for one hour before fetching a fresh one.

<Note>
  TTL is a cache duration, not an instant global switch. After you publish a change, resolvers that already cached the old value will continue using it until their cached copy expires. If you plan to change a record (for example, during a server migration), lower the TTL a day or two in advance so that cached copies expire quickly once you make the change.
</Note>

Higher TTL values reduce DNS query load and improve resilience if the nameserver is temporarily unreachable. Lower TTL values allow faster propagation of changes.

### Comment

The **Comment** field is a free-text note attached to the record. Use it to document what a record is for, when it was added, or any other reference information. Comments are not published to DNS and are only visible in the portal.

## Saving and Publishing Changes

Making edits in the portal does not automatically update the live DNS. There are two distinct steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Save each record individually">
    After editing a record row, click **Save** on that row to write the change to the zone database. Click **Cancel** to discard the edit without saving.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply Changes to publish">
    Once you have saved all the records you want to change, click **Apply Changes** at the top or bottom of the zone editor.

    <Warning>
      Changes are **not published to the live internet** until you click **Apply Changes**. Saving a record only stores it locally in the portal. You must apply changes to replicate them across the EDIS Anycast nameservers.
    </Warning>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the published records">
    After applying, verify your changes using a DNS lookup tool (e.g., `dig` or an online DNS checker) against an authoritative nameserver such as `ns1.edis.global`. Remember that resolvers caching the previous answer will still serve the old record until the TTL expires.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## DNSSEC

The lock icon in the PowerDNS-Admin zone view controls DNSSEC signing for your zone. When DNSSEC is enabled, the portal signs your zone records with a cryptographic key, and a corresponding DS (Delegation Signer) record must be present at the parent zone (your registrar) for the chain of trust to be complete.

<Note>
  Check with EDIS support whether EDIS automatically provisions the DS record at the registrar for your domain, or whether you need to submit it manually. After enabling DNSSEC, verify that the zone validates correctly using a DNSSEC validation tool.
</Note>

If you change nameservers, the existing DNSSEC signing and DS records must be coordinated carefully — an outdated DS record at the registrar can cause DNSSEC-validating resolvers to return `SERVFAIL` for your domain.

## Checking the Authoritative Zone

Always make DNS changes in the portal that controls the **authoritative nameservers** for your domain. If your domain delegates to `ns1.edis.global` and `ns2.edis.global`, changes made here take effect. If your domain is delegated elsewhere, changes here have no effect on public DNS resolution. Verify your current nameserver delegation before editing.

## Backing Up Your Zone Before Making Changes

Before making extensive edits, document your existing records — note each hostname, record type, value, priority, and TTL. EDIS does not guarantee that historical zone snapshots are available on demand. If you need to retrieve a previous zone state, contact [hosting@edis.at](mailto:hosting@edis.at) to check availability.
