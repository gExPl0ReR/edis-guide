Transferring a domain to EDIS is straightforward when you follow the correct order of operations. The key principle is to prepare everything at EDIS *before* submitting the AUTH (transfer authorisation) code — this way your website, email, and DNS continue to work without interruption throughout the transfer process.

## Before You Begin

Gather the following before starting:

- Your current domain's **AUTH code** (also called EPP code or transfer key) — available from your current registrar
- Login access to your [EDIS customer portal](https://manage.edis.at/client/)
- Login access to the [EDIS vHost management panel](https://manage.edis.at/vhost/)
- Contact details for your current registrar (in case you need to unlock the domain or disable transfer locks)

## Transfer Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Order the domain as a transfer at EDIS">
    In the EDIS customer portal at [manage.edis.at/client](https://manage.edis.at/client/), order the domain using the **Transfer** option (not a new registration). When prompted for the AUTH code, **do not enter it yet** — you will submit it later once everything is prepared.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up your email addresses">
    Log in to the [vHost management panel](https://manage.edis.at/vhost/) and create all the email addresses and mailboxes you need under your domain. Configure any aliases or forwarders at this stage.

    <Note>
      If you are migrating IMAP mailboxes, use the **IMAP Sync Tool** available in the vHost panel to copy your existing emails across to EDIS before the transfer completes. For POP3 accounts, the amount of email retained on the old server depends on your mail client settings — back up any locally stored email before proceeding.
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure DNS at EDIS">
    Log in to the EDIS DNS panel at [pdns.edis.global](https://pdns.edis.global/login) and set up all DNS records for your domain — including A/AAAA records for your website, MX records for email, and any TXT records (SPF, DKIM, verification entries, etc.).

    Test that the DNS zone is correct by querying the EDIS nameservers directly before switching anything over.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Notify EDIS support to activate the nameservers">
    Once your DNS zone is ready, contact EDIS support so they can activate the EDIS nameservers for your domain:

    📧 [hosting@edis.at](mailto:hosting@edis.at)

    EDIS will confirm when the nameservers are active and ready to be entered at your current registrar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lower the TTL values at your current registrar">
    At your current DNS provider, reduce the TTL (Time To Live) of all DNS records to a short value — for example **10 minutes (600 seconds)**. Do this well in advance of switching nameservers so that the shorter TTL propagates before you make the change. This minimises the window during which cached DNS records could point to the old server.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point the nameservers to EDIS at your current registrar">
    In your current registrar's control panel, update the authoritative nameservers for your domain to:

    ```
    ns1.edis.global
    ns2.edis.global
    ```

    Wait for DNS propagation and verify that your website and email are resolving correctly via the EDIS nameservers before proceeding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the AUTH code">
    Once your website, DNS, and email are all confirmed to be working correctly via EDIS infrastructure, submit the AUTH code through the agreed secure channel. Contact EDIS support at [hosting@edis.at](mailto:hosting@edis.at) to coordinate the handover of the code.

    <Warning>
      Never send your AUTH code by unencrypted email or in a public message. Agree on a secure submission method with EDIS support beforehand.
    </Warning>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the completed transfer">
    After the transfer completes, check the following:
    - Domain delegation and WHOIS reflect EDIS as the new registrar
    - DNS resolution is working correctly (website loads, email is delivered)
    - DNSSEC validation is intact if your domain uses DNSSEC
    - All required subdomains and services are reachable
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Transfer Duration

<Note>
  Transfer duration depends on the **TLD (domain extension)**, the policies of your current registrar, and whether any lock or dispute is in place. For generic TLDs (gTLDs) an automatic release may occur after five calendar days — however, this is not a fixed deadline that starts from the moment you place your order. For `.at` domains, the transfer is considered complete only once the registry confirms it. Ask your current registrar about any applicable lock periods or transfer restrictions before you begin.
</Note>

## DNSSEC Considerations

Do not disable DNSSEC across the board simply because you are changing registrars. Instead, coordinate the following steps carefully:

1. Confirm whether the new zone at EDIS will be signed and how the DS record will be managed.
2. Ensure the old DS record in the parent zone is removed or replaced in a coordinated way — a stale DS record pointing to old keys will cause DNSSEC-validating resolvers to return `SERVFAIL`, making your domain unreachable.
3. After the transfer, verify DNSSEC validation using a tool such as [dnssec-analyzer.verisignlabs.com](https://dnssec-analyzer.verisignlabs.com/).
