Sending a newsletter is fundamentally different from sending business email. A single campaign can dispatch thousands or tens of thousands of messages simultaneously, generating large volumes of bounces, unsubscribe requests, and delivery reports that must be handled automatically. EDIS email infrastructure is purpose-built for reliable business correspondence and transactional email — it is not designed or recommended for mass newsletter distribution.

<Warning>
  **Do not use EDIS shared hosting email for mass mailing.** Sending large-scale newsletters through EDIS mail infrastructure is not supported and can negatively affect the sending reputation of the shared mail servers, impacting all customers. Use a dedicated newsletter service for bulk campaigns.
</Warning>

</Tip>

## Preparing Your Domain for Newsletter Sending

Before your first send, verify your domain with your chosen newsletter service. This involves adding DNS records to your EDIS DNS zone:

<Steps>

### Get the required DNS records from your newsletter service

After adding your domain in the newsletter platform, it will provide specific DNS records to verify domain ownership and enable authenticated sending. Common record types include:

- **SPF TXT record** — authorises the service's mail servers to send on behalf of your domain
- **DKIM TXT record** — adds a cryptographic signature to outgoing messages
- **CNAME record** — may be required for DKIM key hosting or tracking links

### Add the records to your EDIS DNS zone

Log in to the [EDIS DNS portal](https://pdns.edis.global/login) and add the records exactly as specified by your newsletter service. Use the values provided for **your** domain — example values from documentation or screenshots will not work.

### Merge SPF records — do not create duplicates

Your domain may already have an SPF TXT record for EDIS mail servers. Do not create a second SPF record for the same domain name. Instead, merge the newsletter service's include mechanism into your existing SPF record:

```
v=spf1 include:spf.edis.at include:newsletter-service.example.com ~all
```

Replace `newsletter-service.example.com` with the actual include value your newsletter platform requires.

### Verify DNS propagation

Wait for DNS changes to propagate (typically a few minutes with EDIS Anycast DNS, up to 48 hours in rare cases). Use your newsletter platform's built-in verification tool to confirm the records are active before sending.

### Test before your first real send

Send a test campaign to a small set of internal addresses first. Confirm that SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all pass by inspecting the message headers or using a tool such as [mail-tester.com](https://www.mail-tester.com/). Only proceed to the full list after a clean test result.

</Steps>

## Deliverability Recommendations

A dedicated service is only as effective as the domain reputation behind it. Follow these practices to maintain good deliverability:

- **Only send to opted-in recipients.** Purchased or scraped lists result in high spam complaint rates that damage your sending reputation permanently.
- **Process bounces and unsubscribes immediately.** Most newsletter services do this automatically — make sure the feature is enabled.
- **Warm up a new sending domain gradually.** Start with small volumes and increase over several weeks before sending to your full list.
- **Monitor blacklist status.** Reputation and blacklist status must be checked for the actual sending service's IP addresses — not EDIS mail servers.
- **Set DMARC to at least `p=none` with a monitoring address** so you receive reports about how your domain is being used in email.

## Sending Limits

EDIS Business Email plans have sending limits appropriate for business correspondence. These limits are per plan and are not designed for newsletter volumes. Your newsletter service's own limits, pricing tiers, and rate caps are separate from anything at EDIS — check the service's documentation for its current limits.

A planned send time does not guarantee simultaneous delivery to all recipients. Large sends are queued and delivered progressively by the newsletter service.
