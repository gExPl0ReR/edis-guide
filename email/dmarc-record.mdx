DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance) is a DNS-based email authentication policy that builds on SPF and DKIM. It tells receiving mail servers what to do when a message claims to be from your domain but fails authentication checks — and it lets you receive reports about who is sending email in your domain's name. Adding a DMARC record is an important step towards protecting your domain from spoofing and phishing abuse.

<Note>
  DMARC requires that **SPF and/or DKIM** are already configured and working for your domain before it can function correctly. Set up your [SPF record](/email/spf-record) first, and optionally DKIM, before adding a DMARC record.
</Note>

## How DMARC works

When a recipient's mail server receives a message claiming to be from your domain, it performs SPF and DKIM checks. DMARC adds an alignment check: the domain used in those checks must match the visible `From:` address in the email. If both checks fail alignment, the receiving server follows the policy you have published in your DMARC record — `none`, `quarantine`, or `reject`.

DMARC also sends aggregate reports to a reporting address you specify, giving you visibility into who is sending email using your domain name.

## The DMARC DNS record

Add a TXT record to your DNS zone with the following values:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name / Host** | `_dmarc` |
| **Type** | `TXT` |
| **Value** | See example below |

### Starting DMARC record (recommended)

Begin with a monitoring-only policy (`p=none`) so you can review reports without affecting mail delivery:

```dns
_dmarc TXT "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com"
```

Replace `dmarc@yourdomain.com` with a real, working email address where you want to receive DMARC aggregate reports. Do not use the example address verbatim.

### Explanation of record fields

| Tag | Example value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `v=DMARC1` | — | Required. Identifies this as a DMARC record. |
| `p=none` | `none` / `quarantine` / `reject` | Policy for failing messages: observe only, send to spam, or reject outright. |
| `rua=mailto:...` | Your report address | Address where aggregate reports are sent (optional but highly recommended). |

### Progressing to a stricter policy

Once you have reviewed reports and confirmed that all legitimate sending services pass SPF or DKIM alignment, you can tighten the policy:

```dns
_dmarc TXT "v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com"
```

Then, once you are confident:

```dns
_dmarc TXT "v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com"
```

<Warning>
  Do not jump straight to `p=reject` without first reviewing DMARC reports with `p=none`. A misconfigured policy at `reject` can cause legitimate email from your domain (including newsletters, CRMs, or shared sending services) to be silently dropped by receiving servers.
</Warning>

## Understanding DMARC reports

Aggregate reports (sent to your `rua` address) are XML files summarising authentication results across all mail claiming to be from your domain. Key things to look for:

- **Unknown sending sources** — IP addresses you don't recognise sending email as your domain.
- **SPF/DKIM failures** — legitimate services that are not yet correctly authorised.
- **Alignment issues** — services where the technical sender domain doesn't match your visible `From:` domain.

A report arriving in your inbox is not itself an error — it is a data packet. Use a DMARC report analyser (many free options exist online) to read the XML in a human-friendly format.

## DKIM: a note on DNS keys vs. active signing

Publishing a DKIM public key in DNS does not by itself activate DKIM signing. The signing happens on the outgoing mail server. If you use an external sending service, that service provides the specific DKIM selector and DNS record values for your account — follow their exact instructions. For EDIS mail sending specifically, contact [hosting@edis.at](mailto:hosting@edis.at) to confirm current DKIM support for your setup.

## Where to add the record

Add the record in the authoritative DNS zone for your domain. With EDIS nameservers, use the [EDIS DNS portal](https://pdns.edis.global/login). For a third-party DNS provider, log in to their control panel and create the TXT record with Name `_dmarc` and the value above.
